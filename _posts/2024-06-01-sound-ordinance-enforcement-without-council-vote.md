Troy's Nighthawks Block Party, a downtown charity fundraiser that had run for years, was canceled in June 2024 after organizers cited disputes with the mayor's office. The cancellation was the first public signal that the Mantello administration was applying a new standard to outdoor music, one the council had never voted on.

By summer 2024, the mayor's office was enforcing a 9 PM cutoff for outdoor music at downtown bars and restaurants. The administration described it as an application of "existing guidelines." Business owners described it as a new restriction in everything but name. Outdoor music had not been enforced under the existing code before Mantello took office.

The economic stakes were real. Business owners told the city that 11 to 13 percent of restaurant revenue and roughly half of bar income comes after 9 PM. Troy's downtown corridor had between 180 and 237 establishments affected. Musicians began moving their bookings to Albany.

By January 2026, Gigi Sweets, a Lansingburgh homeowner and music venue advocate, put it in front of the new city council at its first public forum. The cutoff had been walked back from 9 PM to 10 PM under pressure, but the damage was underway. "It's killing us," she said. The council had never taken a vote. "Without a council vote" was her phrase. It was also the correct description of what had happened.

The administration had imposed an enforcement standard affecting hundreds of businesses and the economic life of downtown Troy through a unilateral interpretation of existing code. No ordinance was amended. No public hearing was held. No council member voted yes or no.
