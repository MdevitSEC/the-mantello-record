Dylan Spring lasted five months. He walked out of a Finance Committee meeting mid-presentation on June 20, 2024, did not return to work, and submitted a two-week notice on June 25. His resignation took effect July 9. The seat stayed empty for roughly six months.

Mantello had started her term in January 2024 without a comptroller in place. Her predecessor Andy Petroski departed at the transition, and she made no hire during the handover period, the first time that had happened in 40 years. Spring was brought on in February 2024 and immediately inherited a set of books that no one outside the prior administration knew how to reconcile. The city's KVS financial software required a manual "spin-off" process that only Petroski had understood; without him, no one could close the 2023 accounts.

**What the council saw before he left**

On May 23, 2024, Spring presented Q4 2023 to the Finance Committee, about five months after the quarter ended. The report was incomplete. ProNexus, the outside accounting firm brought in to reconstruct the books, had filed the FY 2023 Annual Financial Report but the state later found it unreliable. Cash was off from Pioneer Bank records by $8 to $9 million. Council President Steele, who had been on the council for five years, said she had never seen the city's finances in this condition. "It's scary to me that we are in this situation, because we've done so well with our finances."

On June 20, the Q1 2024 report arrived five minutes before the Finance meeting. There had been no advance notice. Steele said it was the first time in five years that the Q1 report had not been available for review before the meeting. A motion to defer failed. Spring presented on the spot. Then he left mid-meeting and did not come back.

**The vacancy**

After Spring's departure, the city had no comptroller heading into budget season. Treasurer Gabrielle Mahoney managed daily financial operations. BST & Co., a separate outside firm, served as accounting consultant. Roughly 20 candidates were interviewed for the comptroller seat over the summer and fall. Several withdrew after watching city council meetings.

Q2 2024 was never formally presented. Residents asked about it at the August 8, September 5, and September 19 Finance meetings. No answer was given at any of them. By late September, Steele put the situation plainly: "We have not received any quarterly reports. We don't have a comptroller. It's like we know nothing about the finances." Mayor Mantello promised a hire within two weeks. No hire came.

In August, the council voted to raise the comptroller salary from $103,966 to a range of $125,000 to $150,000 to improve recruitment. The city eventually spent more than $260,000 on outside accounting firms during the gap. Troy received no formal quarterly financial report for the entirety of 2024.
