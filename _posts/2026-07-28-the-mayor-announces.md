"Mayor Carmella R. Mantello today announced..."

That sentence leads 226 official City of Troy press releases. Before you read it again, you should know what it is doing.

---

## Move One: Rename It, Own It

Routine government functions do not make news. They make news when they get a name.

The Department of Public Works crews who fill potholes every spring filled them under the prior mayor too. Under Mantello, the same work became "Operation Pothole" and "Operation Refresh 2024," announced with a personal mayoral quote and weekly press updates.

The police enforcement tactics that predated her became "Project Sentinel," launched with a press conference and tracked with a crime statistics release.

The DPW workers who maintain Troy's streets became "the Quality-of-Life Action Crews."

The naming convention is not accidental. "Operation Refresh." "Operation Pothole." "Operation Sweep." "Project Sentinel." Every one follows a military naming structure, the same format used for Operation Desert Storm, Operation Enduring Freedom, the Manhattan Project.

That structure does specific work beyond claiming ownership.

Military operations have commanders. By naming routine maintenance as operations, Mantello positions herself as the commander rather than the administrator. A department head runs a schedule. A commander runs an operation.

Military operations are mounted against something. Potholes are not scheduled for repair under this framing. They are targeted. The city is not being maintained. It is being defended.

Military operations have a launch and a conclusion. They are events, not ongoing functions. That structure lets her announce the launch, take credit for the result, and step away from the middle: the weeks of actual execution where delays and problems occur. If Operation Pothole fills the potholes on schedule, she won. If it runs behind, that is a departmental update, not a mayoral announcement.

The DPW crews who fill those potholes do not issue battle reports. They do their jobs. The operation is the press release, not the work.

Nothing about the underlying work changed. The workers, the routes, the schedules: the same. What changed is the name on the announcement.

When you rename a government function, you claim it. "Operation Refresh" belongs to whoever named it. "The Department of Public Works spring cleaning" belongs to the department. One press release converts a city employee's routine workday into a mayoral initiative. The worker did not change jobs. Their work got a new owner.

---

## Move Two: Make It Personal

A mayor who manages a department could say: "The Department of Public Works will begin paving on Monday." That is a factual announcement.

That is not what Mantello does.

She writes a fresh personal quote for every release. Every paving update. Every swearing-in. Every grant received. In release after release she is "thrilled," "excited," "honored," "proud."

Mayor Madden's paving quotes were identical across every release, word for word, thanking "the workers of our Department of Public Works, who put themselves in harm's way." ([#70](https://www.troyny.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/70), [#75](https://www.troyny.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/75), [#105](https://www.troyny.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/105), among others.) A standing acknowledgment. The department did this. The mayor knows about it.

In Mantello's releases, the language is always fresh and always personal. The emotion signals that she is a participant in each event, not an administrator who signed off on it. The DPW crew laying asphalt at 6 AM is not the subject. She is.

Freshness of language performs ownership. Boilerplate says the department did it. A personal quote says she did.

---

## Move Three: Make It Feel Like Family

At the swearing-in of three new police officers in June 2024, as officers' mothers pinned their badges, Mantello said: "As the daughter of a Troy police officer, I understand the sacrifices these men and women must make." ([#221](https://www.troyny.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/221))

At the bid opening for the Little Italy Marketplace in February 2026: "My father grew up in this neighborhood and would tell me stories about how the marketplace once thrived." ([#353](https://www.troyny.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/353))

These credentials appear at ceremonial moments, when the audience's own identity is most visible. The signal is that her connection to Troy is not institutional. It is personal. She doesn't just run this city. She belongs to it.

Notice where this credential does not appear: crime statistics releases, accountability statements, or the city's response to the Alalkawi family, whose son was killed in a crash with a Troy police officer. The connection to policing is invoked at the ceremony. It is withheld from the aftermath.

The credential is a tool. It is deployed when it helps and put away when it would not.

---

## What Those Three Moves Build

Taken together, those three moves construct a specific story about who Carmella Mantello is. It runs across all 226 releases.

**She is present.** Nothing happens in Troy that she does not appear to have caused. Events do not occur. She announces them.

**She cares.** Every release carries a fresh personal emotion. She is not an administrator. She is invested.

**She belongs here.** Her roots are in the neighborhoods she governs. She is one of us.

**She is in control.** Everything happens under her plan, her administration, her commitment. Programs do not emerge from departments or council votes. They emerge from her.

**The numbers prove it.** Arrests. Lead pipes replaced. Credit ratings. Marina seasons. Each release adds to the record. By release 226, the story has 225 prior releases behind it.

By release 226, this story does not feel like a story. It feels like the record.

This is the quiet kind of propaganda. Not a rally. Not an attack ad. A calm official announcement, every four days, for 30 months, covering every category of city business, until the description of who governs Troy feels like the only description that has ever existed.

You do not notice it happening. That is the point.

---

## What the Story Erases

Every story that centers one person requires erasing others. This one requires specific erasures.

Troy's credit rating went up under Mantello. That is true. Here is what her press release did not say: that rating was built on eight consecutive years of balanced budgets under Mayor Madden, years when the city spent only what it brought in. Her release described it as "a significant achievement for our administration." ([#229](https://www.troyny.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/229)) The Comptroller who managed those finances is not named. The seven years that earned the rating are not mentioned. The achievement belongs to the administration. She is the administration.

Troy's east side parks were improved under Mantello. That is true. Here is what her press releases did not say: the $2.235 million that paid for those improvements was budgeted by Madden before he left office. He titled his own announcement "Madden-Era ARPA Investments to Improve Parks in 2024." ([#149](https://www.troyny.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/149)) Her releases announced the completed work. His name does not appear.

Troy received $4.3 million in state funding for neighborhood revitalization. That is true. Here is what her press release did not say: someone on city staff wrote the application, managed the relationship with Albany, and tracked the reporting requirements. That person does not exist in the announcement. The governor who sent the money is mentioned once in passing. The mayor who announced it is the headline. ([#351](https://www.troyny.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/351))

Patrick Madden governed this city for eight years. He built the fiscal record she inherited. He allocated the federal money she is spending. He is named zero times across her 226 press releases, including releases that announce his programs, spend his money, and take credit for his work.

The story cannot include him. If he exists, it is not her achievement. If the prior eight years exist, she did not start from zero. The story requires his absence.

---

## What It Is Not

It is not lying.

The street was paved. The grant came through. The credit rating went up. These are real things a real city government did.

That is exactly what makes this work. You cannot fact-check a framing. "Mayor Mantello announced the park improvements" is a true sentence. The parks were improved. She announced them. Both facts are accurate.

What is missing is not a lie. It is a selection: who funded the work, who planned it, who built it, whose eight years made it possible, and who did it before she arrived and got none of the credit. That selection, made 226 times, across every category of city business, by a mayor running for re-election, using a press office funded by your tax dollars, is propaganda.

Not loud propaganda. Not crude propaganda. The kind that works precisely because it sounds like a city government doing its job.

---

## What To Do With This

You have just seen three specific moves. You will see them again.

When she renames a routine function and announces it: that is Move One. Ask what that work was called before she arrived.

When the quote is fresh and personal and emotional: that is Move Two. Ask who actually did the work the quote is claiming.

When she invokes her father or her roots: that is Move Three. Ask where that credential appears when accountability is required.

When she announces a credit rating: ask whose fiscal record earned it.

When she announces a park renovation: ask who funded it and when.

When she announces a crime statistic: ask what the number was before she arrived.

The phrase "The Mayor Announces" is not a neutral description of city government. It is a claim, repeated 226 times with your tax dollars, about who is responsible for what happens in Troy.

Mayor Madden credited Carmella Mantello by name in his press releases when she was Council President. She has never named him in hers.
