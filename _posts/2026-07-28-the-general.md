Generals do not fill potholes. They name the operation, attend the press conference, and accept the credit. The troops do the work. The commander takes the victory.

Carmella Mantello does not pave streets. She does not replace lead pipes. She does not write grant applications or manage the city's bond rating or schedule bulk pickup routes. The Department of Public Works does. The Department of Public Utilities does. The Comptroller does. Career city staff do.

What she does is announce it.

---

## The Myth

The myth of the active mayor runs like this: Troy was struggling. Carmella arrived. Things got done. She needs you to know this. Once every four days, she issues a press release to remind you. Every paving update. Every swearing-in. Every grant received. Every pothole filled. Carmella was there. Carmella made it happen. Carmella is good.

The myth is not built from lies. The streets were paved. The lead pipes are being replaced. The credit rating went up. These are real things.

The myth is built from a specific kind of silence. The people who actually did those things do not exist in the record. The departments that planned them are not named. The prior administration that funded them is not mentioned. The staff who wrote the applications, managed the contractors, and showed up at 6 AM to do the physical work are absent from every announcement.

What remains, after that silence, is Carmella.

---

## How a City Actually Works

A mayor does not run a city the way a general runs an army. A city runs on departments, career staff, and institutional memory that predates any elected official and outlasts all of them.

The Department of Public Works employs the people who pave Troy's streets. They have supervisors, schedules, equipment, and institutional knowledge built over years. They operated before Carmella took office. They will operate after she leaves.

The Comptroller manages the city's finances. The eight consecutive balanced budgets that earned Troy its credit rating upgrade were built and maintained by that office, under a prior administration, before Carmella arrived.

The city staff who wrote the grant application that brought $4.3 million in state neighborhood revitalization funding did so by managing relationships with Albany over months or years. That work does not appear in the press release. The mayor who announced the award does.

Departments do the work. Mayors set priorities and manage politics. Carmella's press office has spent 30 months erasing that distinction.

---

## What the General Claims

In Carmella's record, nothing happens without her. Paving begins when she announces it. Lead pipes get replaced because of her action plan. The credit rating rose because of the Administration's fiscal discipline. Parks were renovated because she delivered.

Each of these claims has a missing subject: the person or department that actually did the thing.

"Mayor Mantello today announced the completion of lead pipe replacements on..." The crew that did the replacements is not in the sentence.

"Under Mayor Mantello's leadership, Troy's credit rating has been upgraded..." The Comptroller who managed the finances is not in the sentence.

"Mayor Mantello secured $4.3 million in state funding for..." The staff member who wrote the application is not in the sentence.

The active mayor of these releases is not a person doing work. She is a grammatical subject, placed at the front of every sentence describing work that other people did.

---

## What Generals Are For

Mayors set budgets. They make appointments. They negotiate with Albany. They make political decisions about what gets funded and what does not. That work is real.

It is not the work in these press releases. The work in these releases belongs to the people who are not named in them: the physical labor, the professional expertise, the institutional execution.

The myth of the active mayor requires their absence. If the DPW superintendent is named, he did it. If the prior administration's fiscal record is acknowledged, she inherited it. If the grant writer is credited, she did not secure it.

The general needs the troops to disappear into the victory. Otherwise it is not a victory. It is a report from the field.

---

## What to Look For

Every press release Carmella issues follows the same grammatical rule: she is the subject, city government is the predicate, and the people who did the work are not in the sentence.

When you read "Mayor Mantello today announced..." ask one question: who did the work that is being announced?

The answer is almost never in the release. That absence is the myth. The myth is the point.
