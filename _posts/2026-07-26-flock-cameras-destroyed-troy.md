Three Flock Safety license plate readers were [destroyed overnight Thursday in Troy](https://cbs6albany.com/news/local/flock-license-plate-reader-cameras-vandalized-in-troy-wrgb), their poles cut in half. Mayor Carmella Mantello and Police Chief Dan DeWolf issued a joint statement Friday calling it "the latest in a disturbing pattern of vandalism that has targeted city property, including panhandling signage, City vehicles, City Hall itself, and even private businesses."

The statement is not wrong as a description of events. It is a gift.

The Troy City Council spent the first half of 2026 pursuing legitimate oversight of the Flock contract through two tracks. In March, the Democratic Council [attempted to end the contract outright](https://cbs6albany.com/news/local/flock-license-plate-reader-cameras-vandalized-in-troy-wrgb). Mantello declared a public safety emergency to keep the cameras running. The Council and the city auditor [filed suit](https://cbs6albany.com/news/local/flock-license-plate-reader-cameras-vandalized-in-troy-wrgb).

The two sides reached a partial agreement in May: restrictions on immigration enforcement use and a requirement for annual audits. Council member Noreen McKee, chair of the Public Safety Committee, [introduced a local law](https://www.wamc.org/news/2026-05-27/troy-council-flock-camera-regulations) requiring deletion of camera data within 48 hours. The Council [paused action on that legislation May 27](https://www.wamc.org/news/2026-05-27/troy-council-flock-camera-regulations) while it gathered police data on how frequently crimes are reported more than 48 hours after they occur. That process was working.

Three destroyed camera poles change none of the underlying questions. The Flock contract was executed through an emergency declaration that bypassed the Council. The 48-hour data deletion provision is still pending. The McKee legislation is still on the table. [Roughly two dozen Flock cameras](https://www.wamc.org/news/2026-05-27/troy-council-flock-camera-regulations) operate throughout Troy. The question of who controls that data, and for how long, was being answered through the democratic process.

What changed is the story. Mantello now holds a joint statement with her police chief, a pattern-of-vandalism narrative, and a visible opposition to point to. DeFlock Troy, the group pushing for camera removal, [acknowledged Friday](https://cbs6albany.com/news/local/flock-license-plate-reader-cameras-vandalized-in-troy-wrgb) that the mayor's emergency declaration has prevented the Council from terminating the contract. That is an accurate and important point. It will not be the sentence anyone clips from their statement.

This publication does not support the destruction of city property. It does not believe property destruction advances the goal of placing the Flock contract under democratic accountability. The Council was the mechanism for that accountability. It was using it.

The mayor's emergency declaration remains in effect. The Council's lawsuit challenging it is unresolved. The cameras destroyed this week were never legally settled. They were simply still standing.
