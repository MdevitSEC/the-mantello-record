## The Charter Violation

Troy City Charter § C-36 is plain: "The Mayor may designate a qualified person to serve in a temporary capacity in any appointive office, but no such position shall be filled in a temporary capacity for more than 90 days."

Richard "Rick" Morrissey has served as Acting Corporation Counsel since at least December 2024. As of mid-2026, that is at minimum 18 months in a temporary capacity. The charter allows 90 days. No confirmation hearing has been held. No formal nomination has been submitted. The position has not been posted.

The person responsible for the city's legal compliance is operating in violation of the city's foundational legal document.

## Background

Before becoming Acting Corporation Counsel, Morrissey served as Deputy Corporation Counsel under confirmed Corporation Counsel Dana Salazar. When Salazar left in mid-to-late 2024, Morrissey stepped back into the lead role, this time without a confirmation hearing.

Morrissey is a private attorney in solo practice at 64 Second Street in Troy. He attended the University at Albany. He was admitted to the bar in Massachusetts in 1992 and New York in 1995. His Massachusetts bar registration is now listed as retired. His New York license is active with no disciplinary history on record. His practice covers municipal law, litigation, real estate, and contract disputes.

The last confirmed Corporation Counsel was Dana Salazar, approved by unanimous city council vote on February 22, 2024. She stated at her confirmation that she would prioritize FOIL requests. She departed mid-to-late 2024 and later announced a run for New York State Supreme Court. Morrissey returned to the city's legal function without a council vote, without a formal appointment, and without a posted position. Under § C-36, the clock started. It expired 90 days later. He is still there.

## What the Role Requires

Corporation Counsel is the city's chief legal officer: responsible for all municipal litigation, advising departments on contract and labor law, overseeing legal compliance across city operations, and managing the city's active legal matters. The position requires council confirmation.

## The Record in the Role

At the April 23, 2026 law committee meeting, Deputy Mayor Donnelly acknowledged the city's legal office runs on one attorney. "Please go find me another attorney that wants to come here for $90,000 a year." He called it "a problem we want to solve." It has not been solved.

At the same meeting, Morrissey testified he could not recall the last time he received any training on Freedom of Information Law. He is the person who trained Communications Director Alex Horton on which FOIL exemptions apply to which requests. When residents appeal a FOIL denial, the appeal goes to Morrissey: the same attorney who oversaw the original response.

CSEA union representatives testified at the same meeting that they had been unable to reach Morrissey for months on basic labor relations matters. His answer, relayed by Donnelly: he works twelve-hour days.

## The Record in Appeals

At a January 22, 2026 Public Safety Committee meeting, a resident raised unanswered FOIL appeals. Morrissey acknowledged falling behind on them during an illness. Council President Steele pressed on delays lasting "months and months." He said some appeals reflected genuine disputes over scope. The committee asked for improved responsiveness.

FOIL appeals go to Morrissey. There is no one else to appeal to.

## The Appointment That Never Happened

Dana Salazar went through a confirmation hearing. Morrissey has not. Under § C-36, The Administration had 90 days to formally nominate him for confirmation or find someone else. It did neither.

Troy's acting chief legal officer has been operating outside Troy's own law for more than a year.
