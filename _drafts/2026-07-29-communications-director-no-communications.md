Alex Horton is the City of Troy's Communications Director. Under the Freedom of Information Law, he confirmed he has no communications with the media.

That finding raises one question with two answers.

The first answer is that Horton is not doing his job. When Mantello [appointed him in January 2025](https://www.troyny.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/272), the city defined his role as overseeing "public relations, media outreach, digital content, and community engagement initiatives." Mantello called him a person with "a strong commitment to transparent and effective communication." CBS6, WTEN, WAMC, and Spectrum News cover Troy regularly. The city has issued 226 press releases under Carmella Mantello. Those releases go somewhere. If Horton sent them and received nothing back, texted no one, exchanged no messages with any reporter or producer at any outlet over six months, he is not communicating. He holds the title. He does not appear to be doing the work.

Government transparency runs through the media. A [Director of Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_communications) is defined as the organization's "key spokesperson and media contact." That is the job. Mantello's stated reason for hiring Horton was "transparent and effective communication with our residents." Under FOIL, the record of that communication is zero.

The second answer is that he is hiding his communications. On July 16, 2026, this publication filed FOIL Reference No. C008481-071626, requesting all texts, iMessages, and electronic messages sent or received by Horton on any city-issued or personal device between January 1, 2026 and the date of filing, with any representative of CBS6, WTEN, WAMC, Spectrum News, or any other media organization. The request was denied the same day. The denial arrived by automated email. It identified no person by name or title. That omission violated 21 NYCRR 1401.2, which requires that the records access officer be identified in any FOIL response.

This publication appealed. Acting Corporation Counsel Richard T. Morrissey denied the appeal on July 28 and described what the search consisted of: "All he had to do was look at his phone(s) or other device(s) to determine if there were any records responsive to your request. It appears that he did so promptly and found none. Diligence requires nothing further."

Horton searched his own devices. He reported the result to Morrissey. Morrissey accepted it. No one else reviewed the devices. No one asked a follow-up question. The person with the most interest in the outcome of the search was the one who conducted it.

This publication filed a complaint with the New York State Committee on Open Government. The Committee confirmed the 21 NYCRR 1401.2 violation. On the self-certification, it ruled that any employee performing a search acts as an agent of the agency and that no independent verification is required. The administrative avenue is closed.

Corporation Counsel denied the request. Corporation Counsel denied the appeal.

On July 11, 2026 — five days before the FOIL period closed — the Troy Record published a story on the City Council's override of Mantello's veto on a charter change hearing. Horton is quoted directly: "We just feel it oversteps so far that we can't support a public hearing on something that oversteps that far." On July 24, eight days after the FOIL period closed, the Troy Record cited "correspondence with the city's Communications Director Alex Horton" on the Flock camera vandalism.

The FOIL asked for all electronic communications between Horton and any media organization from January 1 through July 16, 2026. A reporter had his words on July 11. The search found nothing.

One of the two answers is true. The city did not try to find out which.
