Mayor Carmella Mantello (R) has served as Troy's mayor since January 2024. Below is the complete record of documented controversies, sourced from local news, public meeting transcripts, and official city records.

---

## 2026

### [July 9, 2026 — Carmella Is Pushing Back Against a Survey](/2026/07/09/mckee-street-cleaning-survey/)

Noreen McKee sent a survey to residents and businesses asking whether five-day-a-week street sweeping is necessary. Carmella called it an overreach. The parking restrictions have been in place since the 1990s. She did not create them.

---

### [June 25, 2026 — Council Moves to Limit Emergency Powers in 11-Minute Meeting; Mantello at Cookout Fundraiser](/2026/06/25/mantello-skips-charter-meeting-fundraiser/)

The council called a special meeting to limit Carmella's emergency powers. It lasted 11 minutes. She was at a cookout fundraiser. The charter requires her to attend all special meetings.

---

### [June 20, 2026 — Troy's FOIL Officer Got One Training Session. Appeals Go to the Same Person Who Denied You.](/2026/06/20/troy-foil-transparency/)

Alex Horton's only FOIL training was a single software walkthrough. He cannot recall who conducted it. When residents appeal a denial, it goes to the same attorney who oversaw the original response. The State Committee on Open Government was "dumbfounded" by the city's denials.

---

### [May 11, 2026 — City Council Files Lawsuit Against Mantello Over Flock Emergency](/2026/05/11/city-council-files-lawsuit-flock-emergency/)

The city council and city auditor sued Carmella over her April 1 emergency declaration. She used emergency powers to authorize a $78,000 vendor payment the council and auditor had blocked. It was her tenth emergency declaration since taking office. "Policy disputes are resolved through the democratic lawmaking process, not at the whims of a single chief executive."

---

### [April 9, 2026 — Good Cause Eviction Veto Overridden 7-0](/2026/04/09/good-cause-eviction-veto-overridden/)

The council overrode Carmella's veto 7-0. Same margin as the original passage. Every Republican who had blocked the law was already gone. Two-thirds of Troy residents rent.

---

### [April 1, 2026 — Emergency Declaration to Pay Flock Camera Contract](/2026/04/01/emergency-declaration-flock-cameras/)

The council tabled the Flock Safety contract 7-0. The auditor froze payments. Carmella declared a public safety emergency the next morning to pay the $78,000 bill anyway. April Fools' Day.

---

### [March 18, 2026 — Mantello Vetoes Good Cause Eviction](/2026/03/18/mantello-vetoes-good-cause-eviction/)

The council passed Good Cause Eviction 7-0. Carmella vetoed it thirteen days later. "What's the rush?" Every Republican who had blocked a public hearing on the same law was already out of office. In Troy, two-thirds of residents rent.

---

### [March 2026 — ICE Cooperation: Council Acts, Mantello Stays Silent](/2026/03/01/ice-cooperation-mantello-stays-silent/)

In September 2025, Carmella said Troy would "fully cooperate" with federal immigration enforcement. In March 2026, when the new council passed a resolution saying it wouldn't, she called it "solely the Council's political views." The woman who testified against the earlier resolution got a new city job at $80,000 a year.

---

### [February 14, 2026 — Troy Proctor's Foundation Sues to Halt City Hall Conversion](/2026/02/14/proctors-foundation-sues-environmental-review/)

The Troy Proctor's Foundation filed a 91-page lawsuit against the LDC to halt construction inside the former theater. The complaint: no environmental review, asbestos and 1914-era steel beams undisclosed, irreversible damage to a historic building. Carmella: "Emblematic of why Troy hasn't had a real city hall in 15 years."

---

### [January 8, 2026 — Absent from New Council's First Public Meeting](/2026/01/08/absent-new-council-first-public-meeting/)

Twenty-two Trojans showed up to the new council's first public forum. Carmella didn't. Neither did anyone from her administration. "The mayor's absence at the new council's first public meeting is indicative of what the next two years of your term will be."

---

## 2025

### [December 30, 2025 — $6 Million Frear Park Bond Pushed Through Outgoing Republican Council](/2025/12/30/frear-park-bond-outgoing-council/)

The outgoing Republican council's last act: a $6 million bond for Frear Park with no site plan, no construction documents, and a SEQR filing a resident would testify violated state law. A resident called the city clerk at 3 PM that day. No project plans were on file. She had a recording.

---

### [December 17, 2025 — Proctor's Theater Seat Removal Contradicts Preservation Claims](/2025/12/17/proctors-theater-seat-removal/)

Photos hit social media showing every orchestra-level seat pulled from Proctor's Theater. The construction plan: a modern box-in-a-box office chamber built inside the auditorium, with no legal obligation to preserve anything. In 2004, Trojans organized to stop RPI from doing the same thing. They won.

---

### [December 17, 2025 — Mantello Calls Knickerbacker Park Board "Reckless" After 5-Month Non-Response](/2025/12/17/knickerbacker-park-board-reckless/)

The Knick Board submitted a lease proposal in July 2025. The city didn't respond for five months. When the board hired a lawyer, Carmella called them "reckless" and accused them of acting in "bad faith." The city owed them $25,000 in unpaid obligations.

---

### [November 20, 2025 — Bonadio Audit Accepted; Two Years of Financial Failures on Record](/2025/11/20/troy-financial-reporting-failures/)

The FY 2024 audit flagged two material weaknesses. Three comptrollers in 18 months. $260,000 in outside accounting fees. The first on-time annual filing of Carmella's term came April 28, 2025. Steele: "Is that a high bar? I don't really think so, but I'm taking it as good news."

---

### [November 7, 2025 — City Hall Move to Proctor's Approved on 4-3 Party-Line Vote](/2025/11/07/city-hall-move-proctors-approved/)

November 4, Trojans voted out every Republican on the city council. November 7, the outgoing Republican majority voted 4-3 to commit Troy to Proctor's Theater at $675,000 a year for 30 years. The lease was still a draft. Tom Casey: the results were "a national anti-Republican wave."

---

### [Summer–Fall 2025 — NYSERDA Clean Energy Grants Blocked by Administration Non-Response](/2025/09/01/nyserda-grants-blocked-non-response/)

Troy's sustainability task force spent more than a year trying to get recognized by The Administration. No response. The grants waiting: $837,000 in match-free NYSERDA funds and up to $500,000 in EV charging money. Match-free meant no city money required. Just a signature.

---

### [July 2025 — First Quarterly Financial Report in 18 Months](/2025/07/03/first-quarterly-financial-report-18-months/)

State law requires quarterly financial reports. Troy went 18 months without one. When the first arrived in June 2025, it showed the city had already burned through 40 percent of its annual overtime budget by the end of March.

---

### [June–July 2025 — City Hall Lease Dispute: $226K Unpaid, Termination Deadline Missed](/2025/06/13/city-hall-lease-dispute-unpaid-fees/)

Moving city hall to Proctor's created an immediate problem at the old location. The city owed First Columbia $226,807 in Common Area Maintenance fees and disputed the bill. Total exposure: $1.4 million. Settlement: $134,000, full disputed amount, no interest, in exchange for the city supporting the landlord's tax break application.

---

### [May 2025 — Third Comptroller in 18 Months Hired](/2025/05/29/third-comptroller-18-months/)

Michael McNeff was Troy's third comptroller in 18 months. Dylan Spring walked out mid-presentation. Jack Krokos lasted less than three months. The revolving door cost more than $260,000 in outside accounting fees. When the council asked for the acting treasurer's resume, Corporation Counsel said he didn't know if one existed. She was in the hallway.

---

## 2024

### [December 30, 2024 — Last-Minute ARPA Funds Reallocation at Year-End Special Meeting](/2024/12/30/arpa-funds-reallocation/)

The federal deadline to obligate Troy's $42 million in ARPA funds was December 31, 2024. The Administration called a special meeting with one day left. Steele: "I have to in good conscience chastise the administration for the handling of this. We've known about that deadline for years."

---

### [October 10, 2024 — 2025 Budget Submitted Seven Minutes Before Deadline, With Errors](/2024/10/10/budget-submitted-seven-minutes-before-deadline/)

Carmella filed the 2025 budget seven minutes before the legal deadline, with errors. Corrected materials arrived almost an hour later. The document was not searchable, had no index, and no hard copy appeared until the day of the budget meeting. The garbage fee rose from $162 to $252. A "temporary" fee from 2018.

---

### [July 26, 2024 — Planning Commission Abolished, Replaced With Mayor-Appointed Board](/2024/07/26/local-law-3-planning-commission-repeal/)

Bill Keal put it in writing: he supported abolishing the commission because "a new administration wants people who share its vision." Commissioner James Rath read that email aloud at the public hearing while Keal sat at the dais. Every speaker at both hearings opposed the change. The council voted yes. Carmella filled all five new seats herself.

---

### [July 25, 2024 — Expletive-Laced Audio Recording With Downtown Businessman](/2024/07/25/expletive-laced-audio-campbell-avenue/)

WAMC obtained a recording of Carmella in a profanity-laced argument with Anthony DeMeo, whose family has operated Bob DeMeo's Discount Wine and Liquor for 49 years. A city construction project nearly put him out of business. Businesses were never given an accurate timeline. Then, at the end of May 2026, the city went back. Nobody had coordinated the two projects before the first one started.

---

### [July 2024 — Comptroller Dylan Spring Resigns Under Pressure](/2024/07/01/comptroller-dylan-spring-resigns/)

Dylan Spring walked out of a Finance Committee meeting mid-presentation on June 20, 2024, and didn't come back. He lasted five months. Multiple replacement candidates withdrew after watching council meetings. Troy received no quarterly financial report for all of 2024.

---

### [Summer 2024 — Sound Ordinance Enforcement Imposed Without Council Vote](/2024/06/01/sound-ordinance-enforcement-without-council-vote/)

Carmella imposed a 9 PM outdoor music cutoff on downtown businesses. No council vote. No public hearing. No ordinance. The Administration called it "existing guidelines." Business owners called it a new restriction. Musicians booked Albany instead. The Nighthawks Block Party was canceled. Nobody voted on any of it.

---

### [2024 (Ongoing) — Transparency and Information Failures](/2024/01/01/transparency-and-information-failures/)

The pattern across two years: ask, and receive nothing back.

- **FOIL.** One training session. Appeals go to the same attorney who denied you. The State called the Flock denials inexplicable.
- **Labor contracts.** CSEA waited months for a response. Improper practice charges followed.
- **Knick Board.** Five months of silence, then blamed for getting a lawyer.
- **Community proposals.** South Troy housing: two years, no response. Sustainability grants: a year of calls, nobody picked up.
- **Last-minute documents.** The city hall lease was a draft when the council voted. The Frear Park bond had no project plans on file three hours before the vote.
